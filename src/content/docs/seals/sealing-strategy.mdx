## Why this matters

Every joint in a motor is a potential leak path for gas at MEOP and around 3000 K: case-to-closure and segment joints,
nozzle-to-case, the igniter boss, and pressure-transducer and test ports. A leak is rarely a slow loss of pressure — hot
gas cuts a channel through metal in moments. The Challenger accident began at a booster field joint whose O-rings could
not follow the joint as it opened on a cold morning.

The O-ring page sizes a single seal. This page is about the **strategy**: which seal, where, how many, how they are
checked, and how the joint keeps them working.

## Seal types

| Seal | How it seals | Good for | Watch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| O-ring, bore (radial) | Squeezed between bore and spigot; pressure pushes it onto the far groove wall | Closures, nozzle-to-case, igniters | Radial growth of the bore, extrusion gap, cold resilience |
| O-ring, face (axial) | Squeezed in a flange-face groove | Flanged closures, port covers | Flange lift and rotation; depends on bolt preload |
| Back-up rings | Stiff rings beside the O-ring that bridge the clearance | High pressure or larger clearances | Fit on the low-pressure side — both sides if pressure can reverse |
| Metal seals — C, E, metal O-rings, crush gaskets | Elastic or plastic metal contact under a high seating load | High temperature, long storage, aggressive fluids | High bolt load, very fine finish, little springback, usually single-use |
| Port seals — O-ring bosses, bonded washers | An O-ring under a fitting's shoulder | Transducer and test ports, plugs | The plug is part of the seal count |
| Thermal barriers and fillers — putty, RTV, fibre rope | Fill the joint gap ahead of the seal | Keeping hot gas and particles off the elastomer | Not pressure seals on their own; can trap or channel gas |
| Thread sealants — tapered pipe threads, anaerobics | Fill the thread clearance | Low-pressure fittings | Not as the only seal in a critical pressure path |

<SealArrangements />

**Bore or face?** A bore seal sits on a spigot, so it does not care if the flange lifts — but it sees every bit of the
bore's radial growth. A face seal is clamped by the bolts, so it is immune to radial growth but opens as soon as the
flange lifts or rotates. Each is the better choice where the other is weak.

<Analogy title="A doorstop under a door that swells">
  A rubber wedge holds a door shut only while the gap stays the size it was fitted for. If the frame warps open, the
  wedge has to spring out to fill it — and a cold rubber wedge does not spring. Make the frame stiff and the wedge never
  has to move.
</Analogy>

## Redundancy that is real

Critical motor joints carry two seals in series — a primary and a secondary — so one can fail without a leak. That only
works if they do not fail together:

- **Avoid common causes.** Two identical O-rings in the same gland region see the same cold, the same gap opening and the
  same contamination. They are two copies of one seal.
- **Add diversity where you can** — a bore primary with a face secondary, or seals at different stiffness and
  temperature.
- **Verify both.** A secondary that has never been tested is a hope. Leak-check each seal, or the space between them,
  after assembly.
- **Count everything in the path** — port plugs, transducer bosses and the threads of the igniter all need their own
  seal and back-up.

After Challenger, the redesigned Shuttle booster field joint added:
- a capture feature that limits joint rotation;
- a third O-ring;
- an insulation flap that pressure closes against the joint;
- heaters to keep the seals warm.

Nozzle joints later gained a carbon-fibre rope thermal barrier ahead of the O-rings.

## Keeping the gland closed

The best seal is one whose gap never moves. Under pressure:

- **The bore grows.** A thin-walled case grows radially by

$$
\Delta r = \frac{p\,r^2}{E\,t}\left(1 - \frac{\nu}{2}\right)
$$

  — inversely with the wall at the seal seat. Seat bore seals in a thick, stiff joint ring, not in the membrane.
- **The flange lifts or rotates.** Keep the bolted joint well below separation ([Bolted joints](../../joints/bolted-joints/)),
  make flanges stiff, and put face seals inboard of the bolts.
- **The spigot can help** — if the mating part grows with the case, the gap stays shut.
- **Temperature changes everything.** Cold elastomer recovers slowly and shrinks; keep seals in their qualified range,
  with heaters or insulation if needed ([Transient thermal](../../motor/transient-thermal/)).
- **Keep hot gas off the seal.** Insulation, joint fillers and thermal barriers stop gas and particles before they
  reach the O-ring, which then only has to hold pressure.

## Leak-check ports

A port drilled into the space between two seals lets you pressurise and watch the pressure fall:

$$
Q = \frac{V\,\Delta p}{t} \quad\text{(in standard cm}^3\text{/s with } \Delta p \text{ in atmospheres)}
$$

- **Temperature mimics leaks.** The trapped gas changes pressure by $p_{abs}\,\Delta T/T$; a tenth of a degree can
  outweigh the gauge. Stabilise and record temperature, or use a helium mass-spectrometer test for tighter limits.
- **Direction matters.** Pressurising between two seals loads the primary from its back, seating it on the wrong groove
  wall; at ignition it must shuttle across before it seals. Test in the working direction where you can. The Rogers
  Commission also noted that raising the leak-check pressure on the Shuttle field joint could blow holes in its
  insulating putty.
- **Plug and verify the port.** After the test the port is part of the pressure boundary: a sealed plug, its own
  torque, and its own check.

## Worked example — the aft closure seal

The aft closure of the 300 mm case seals on a radial O-ring: 5.33 mm section, with **10.8% minimum squeeze** at the cold
corner after compression set ([O-ring design](../o-ring-design/#worked-example--a-nozzle-to-case-radial-seal)). MEOP is
10 MPa; the closure spigot grows about 0.02 mm.

1. **Seated in the plain cylinder** (1.37 mm wall from [Case sizing](../../motor/case-sizing/)): the case grows
   0.68 mm in radius — its full hoop strain of 0.45%. The gland opens 0.66 mm, but the ring is only compressed 0.58 mm:
   **the seal unloads and leaks**. A bore seal never belongs in the membrane.
2. **In a 10 mm joint ring:** growth 0.093 mm, gland opening 0.073 mm — squeeze 9.4%, below a 10% floor.
3. **The seat wall for 10%** is 14.9 mm: the joint ring is a 15 mm forging, a few hundred grams that make the seal
   work.
4. **Redundancy:** two rings with a port between them. With 2 cm³ between the seals, 700 kPa, a 0.1 kPa gauge and
   10 minutes, the smallest visible leak is $3.3\times10^{-6}$ std cm³/s. But 0.1 °C of drift moves the pressure
   0.27 kPa — larger than the gauge — so temperature control decides the result. For an allowed leak of
   $1\times10^{-5}$ std cm³/s (an example), a 200 s hold would do with stable temperature.
5. **Diversity:** the bolted closure flange from [Bolted joints](../../joints/bolted-joints/) does not separate at
   MEOP (17.7 kN against 32.1 kN), so a **face seal** there would stay shut. A bore primary with a face secondary
   cannot be opened by the same mechanism.

## Where this stops

- **Thin-wall growth only.** At the joint ring, the junction with the dome or the next segment bends and rotates; the
  real gap opening needs a joint analysis or a finite-element model.
- **Static.** At ignition the gap opens in milliseconds; whether a cold ring follows is a
  [viscoelastic](../../fundamentals/viscoelasticity/) question.
- **Pressure-decay physics simplified** — ideal gas, constant volume. Helium permeation through elastomers limits very
  tight helium tests.
- **Erosion and hot-gas flow** through a gap are not modelled; keep gas off the seals instead.

## On the drawing

- **Seals and back-up rings by specification** — size, compound, hardness, and lot traceability.
- **Gland dimensions and surface finish**, and the lubricant.
- **Seal seat wall and joint stiffness** — the dimensions the gap analysis relied on.
- **Leak-test procedure** — pressure, medium, direction, hold time, temperature record and acceptance.
- **Port plugs** — seal, torque and verification. Seals are replaced whenever the joint is opened.
