## Why this matters

A motor is assembled at room temperature and then stored, transported and fired anywhere across its qualified range.
It is built from materials that grow very differently with temperature: a steel or composite case, aluminium skirts and
closures, titanium fittings, rubbery insulation, carbon nozzle parts — and a propellant grain that expands about eight
times as much as steel. Every interface between them is a joint that is squeezed, opened or slid by temperature:
skirts that come loose, bonds that peel at the edges, seal glands that open, bolts that lose preload, grains that crack.

## Free and constrained expansion

A free part simply grows, by $\varepsilon = \alpha\,\Delta T$. Stop it, and the same strain turns into stress:

$$
\sigma = E\,\alpha\,\Delta T \quad \text{held in one direction}
$$

$$
\sigma = \frac{E\,\alpha\,\Delta T}{1-\nu} \quad \text{held in a plane}
$$

For steel over 100 °C, held in one direction, that is $205\,000 \times 12\times10^{-6} \times 100 = 246$ MPa — from
temperature alone. Between two joined parts, what matters is the **mismatch**, $(\alpha_1 - \alpha_2)\,\Delta T$, and
how stiffly the joint makes them share it.

<Analogy title="A kettle thermostat">
  A bimetal strip is two metals bonded face to face. Heat it, and the one that grows more cannot get away from the other,
  so the strip bends. Every bonded or tightly joined pair of materials in a motor is a bimetal strip: the mismatch has to
  go somewhere — into bending, into stress, or into slip.
</Analogy>

## Fits that change with temperature

An inner part of diameter $d$ in an outer part changes its diametral fit by

$$
\Delta\delta = d\,(\alpha_{inner} - \alpha_{outer})\,\Delta T
$$

If the outer part expands more — aluminium round steel — the fit loosens when hot and tightens when cold. The contact
pressure for an interference $\delta$ follows Lamé's thick-cylinder solution:

$$
p = \frac{\delta}{\dfrac{d}{E_o}\left(\dfrac{D^2 + d^2}{D^2 - d^2} + \nu_o\right) + \dfrac{d}{E_i}\left(\dfrac{d^2 + d_i^2}{d^2 - d_i^2} - \nu_i\right)}
$$

with the outer part's diameter $D$ and the inner part's bore $d_i$. So:

- **Check both ends.** The interference must survive the loosening extreme, and the outer part must not yield at the
  tightening one.
- **Do not rely on friction across dissimilar metals** to locate or carry load if the range takes the fit to zero. Pin,
  key, bolt or shoulder the part, and let the fit only centre it.
- **Match the expansion where you can** — titanium inside steel tightens when hot rather than loosening.

## Bonded joints

A thin layer bonded to a thick part takes the full mismatch as an equal-biaxial stress,
$\sigma = \tfrac{E}{1-\nu}(\alpha_{sub} - \alpha_{layer})\,\Delta T$. Between two adherends of similar thickness, the
adhesive transfers the mismatch by shear, and the shear piles up at the **ends** of the overlap (shear lag):

$$
\tau_{max} = \frac{G}{t_a}\,\frac{\Delta\alpha\,\Delta T}{\beta}\tanh\frac{\beta L}{2}
$$

$$
\beta^2 = \frac{G}{t_a}\left(\frac{1}{E_1 t_1} + \frac{1}{E_2 t_2}\right)
$$

- **The peak does not fall with longer overlap** once $L$ is a few times $1/\beta$. The middle of a long bond carries no
  shear at all.
- **A thicker or softer bondline lowers it** — for a long overlap the peak goes as $\sqrt{G/t_a}$ — as do tapered
  ends and closer-matched adherends.
- **Adhesives are viscoelastic.** A slow cool-down relaxes some of the stress; a fast thermal shock does not, which is
  why cold shock is the hard case. See [Viscoelasticity](../../fundamentals/viscoelasticity/).
- **The grain-to-case bond** is the largest bonded mismatch on a motor — worked on the
  [Propellant grain](../../motor/propellant-grain/) page.

## Bolted joints

- **Preload changes with temperature** when the bolt and the clamped parts expand differently — steel bolts in an
  aluminium flange lose preload when cold. See the temperature section of [Bolted joints](../bolted-joints/#temperature).
- **Plates slide against each other.** Two plates of different materials bolted together move relative to each other by
  $\Delta\alpha\,\Delta T\,R$ at a distance $R$ from their common fixed point. Within the hole clearance, friction stores
  that as shear until the joint slips — a source of preload loss and fretting. Beyond it, the outer bolts bear and are
  sheared on every cycle. Use one fixed hole with radially slotted others, or flexures.

## Transients

Uniform temperature is the easy case. During firing, the inner parts of the nozzle and the insulation heat by hundreds of
degrees in seconds while the outer shells stay cool. The hot inner part then grows against a cold outer one —
compression on heating, gaps on cooling. Nozzle throat inserts sit in their housings with designed gaps and compliant
layers for this reason. Use [Transient thermal](../../motor/transient-thermal/) for the temperatures, then this page's
methods for the fit at each moment.

## What it means for a motor

| Interface | Cold | Hot | Design response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Propellant grain on the case | Grain shrinks: bore strain, bond tension | Grain grows: compression | Stress-free temperature, bore shape, stress-relief flaps — [Propellant grain](../../motor/propellant-grain/) |
| Aluminium skirt or closure on a steel case | Grips harder | Loosens | Pin or bolt; the fit only centres |
| Steel or titanium fitting in an aluminium part | Tightens | Loosens | Size the fit for the hot end; inserts |
| Metal ring bonded to a composite case | Ring shrinks onto the composite: edge shear | The reverse | Tapered ends, compliant adhesive, check both extremes |
| Carbon or graphite throat in a steel housing | Housing grips the throat | Throat heats and grows during firing | Designed gaps, compliant layers |
| Seal gland across two materials | Gap shrinks or grows | The opposite | Check squeeze at both ends — [Sealing strategy](../../seals/sealing-strategy/) |
| Bolted flange, aluminium on steel | Preload falls; plates slide | Preload rises | Preload margin at the cold end; slotted holes |

## Worked example — the 300 mm motor from −40 to +60 °C

Assembled at 20 °C, so the cold end is 60 °C below assembly and the hot end 40 °C above.

1. **Aluminium skirt ring on the steel attach ring.** Interface ⌀310 mm, ring OD 330 mm, attach-ring bore 290 mm, 0.10 mm
   interference at assembly, 30 mm long, friction 0.15. The mismatch is $310 \times (12.0 - 23.4)\times10^{-6} =
   -3.5$ μm per degree:
   - **at −40 °C** the interference grows to 0.31 mm — 3.4 MPa of contact pressure, 54 MPa in the aluminium, and the
     fit holds 14.7 kN;
   - **at +60 °C** it becomes a **0.04 mm clearance**: the skirt is loose.

   The interference alone cannot carry the skirt: it must be pinned or bolted. A titanium ring would tighten when hot
   instead, and stays at 0.04 mm interference cold.
2. **Carbon-fibre skirt bonded to the steel case.** CFRP 3 mm thick on the 1.37 mm case wall, an epoxy with a 0.2 mm
   bondline and shear modulus 800 MPa, 50 mm overlap. At −40 °C the adhesive shear peaks at **11.9 MPa** at each end,
   all within about 15 mm of the ends. Doubling the overlap changes nothing; doubling the bondline to 0.4 mm cuts the
   peak by √2, to about 8.4 MPa. Compare with the adhesive's allowable at −40 °C, in cold shock.
3. **Aluminium closure plate bolted to a steel flange.** At the 140 mm bolt circle the plates slide **0.096 mm** relative
   to each other at −40 °C — inside the 0.5 mm hole clearance, but with close-fit holes the outer bolts would bear.
4. **The grain.** Propellant at $100\times10^{-6}$/K on the steel case shrinks 2.6 mm more on diameter than the case
   between its stress-free 60 °C and −40 °C. The [Propellant grain](../../motor/propellant-grain/) page turns that into
   bore strain and bond stress.

## Where this stops

- **Uniform temperature and room-temperature properties.** Modulus, yield and expansion all change across the range,
  and transients need the temperature field from [Transient thermal](../../motor/transient-thermal/).
- **Elastic.** Plastic yielding at a tight fit, creep and adhesive relaxation change the result after the first cycle.
- **One-dimensional bond model.** Real bond ends also see peel stress and a stress singularity; a finite-element model
  or test is needed for allowables.
- **Composites are anisotropic.** Hoop and axial expansion of a filament-wound case differ; use the laminate's values in
  each direction.

## On the drawing

- **Fits at a stated temperature** — the interference or clearance and the temperature it applies at.
- **The positive feature that carries load** — pins, keys, bolts — when the fit can go to zero.
- **Slotted holes and the fixed point** for bolt patterns across dissimilar materials.
- **Bond details** — adhesive, bondline thickness (controlled with spacers or scrim), end taper, surface preparation.
- **The temperature range** the design was checked over.
