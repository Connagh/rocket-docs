## Why this matters

A dimension and a ± tolerance say how big a feature is and roughly where. They do not say how flat, round, square or
coaxial it must be, or where it is measured from — and every inspector, machinist and supplier fills that gap
differently. Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T) closes it: each feature is controlled relative to **datums**
chosen from how the part fits and works, with tolerance zones shaped like the function.

On a motor that is the difference between a seal that keeps its squeeze and one that does not, a closure whose 30 bolts
all start, and a nozzle that sits on the axis. It also saves money: tolerance that the function does not need is given
back, often automatically as the features move away from their tightest size.

<Analogy title="Darts">
  A dart counts by how far it lands from the bull, not by how far left and how far up separately. A ± tolerance on x
  and y is a square box: it rejects a dart in the corner of the circle that would have scored, and accepts nothing it
  should not. A position tolerance is the circle — for the same worst case, 57% more area to land in.
</Analogy>

## The geometric characteristics

<GdtSymbols />

Two rules of thumb pick between them. Use the **least control that guarantees the function**: flatness where a face only
has to seal, perpendicularity where it must also square to an axis, profile where it must also be in the right place.
And prefer controls that **a gauge or a measuring machine can check against the same datums the assembly uses**.

## The feature control frame

<GdtFrame />

The frame reads as a sentence: *the position of these holes must be within a ⌀0.5 cylinder at MMC, relative to datums
A, B at MMB, and C.* The modifiers that most often matter on motor hardware:

- **Ⓜ︎ maximum material condition** — tolerance grows as the feature departs from its tightest size. For clearance:
  bolt holes, pilots that only have to assemble.
- **Ⓛ least material condition** — the reverse, to protect a minimum wall or edge distance.
- **No modifier — regardless of feature size**, the default. For features that locate precisely.
- **Ⓟ projected tolerance zone** — the zone extends above a threaded hole, where the fastener actually is.
- **Ⓕ free state** — for non-rigid parts measured without restraint.

## Datums

A **datum feature** is a real surface on the part; the **datum** is the perfect plane, axis or point it establishes,
in the gauge or fixture that contacts it (the datum feature simulator). Three datums in order of precedence make a
**datum reference frame**:

- **Primary** — the part rests on it at three points: it fixes three degrees of freedom. Usually the largest
  mating face.
- **Secondary** — two points, or the axis of a pilot: two more.
- **Tertiary** — one point, or a clocking hole: the last.

Choose datums from **assembly and function**, not from convenience in the machine shop: the face a closure seats on,
the pilot that centres it, the dowel that clocks it. A datum feature that is itself loosely controlled makes everything
referenced to it loose — so the datum features get their own form controls (flatness of A, perpendicularity of B to A).
When a secondary datum such as a pilot is referenced at **maximum material boundary (MMB)**, the pattern may also shift
by the pilot's clearance — tolerance that only applies if the mating part really has that clearance.

## Size and form

- **ASME: Rule #1.** Unless stated otherwise, a feature of size must have perfect form at MMC: the size limits also
  limit form (the envelope principle).
- **ISO: independency.** Under ISO 8015, size and form are independent unless the envelope requirement Ⓔ is applied.
  The same drawing can mean different things under the two systems — say which one governs.
- **Non-rigid parts.** A thin case wall is not round in the free state; it becomes round when the closure pilot goes
  in. Control it as it works: an average diameter (AVG) and circularity in the restrained condition, with any free-state
  limits marked Ⓕ, and a note saying how it is restrained for inspection (ASME Y14.5; ISO 10579).

## MMC, bonus tolerance and virtual condition

With a feature of size controlled at MMC, the allowed tolerance is the stated value plus the **bonus** — how far the
actual size has moved from MMC:

$$
t_{allowed} = t + |\,d_{actual} - d_{MMC}\,|
$$

Measured position from coordinate deviations is $2\sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2}$. The **virtual condition** is the
worst-case boundary the mating part must clear:

$$
VC_{hole} = MMC - t
$$

$$
VC_{pin} = MMC + t
$$

so a functional gauge is simply a set of pins at the holes' virtual condition, on the datums. For fastener patterns
with largest fastener diameter $F$ and clearance holes at MMC $H$:

- **Floating fasteners** (clearance holes in both parts): each part may have $T = H - F$.
- **Fixed fasteners** (one part threaded): the two share it, $T_1 + T_2 = H - F$ — with a **projected tolerance
  zone** on the threaded holes. Without one, a leaning thread throws the fastener further off over the height $P$ it
  projects, and the hole must grow to $H = F + T_1 + T_2\,(1 + 2P/D)$, with $D$ the least thread engagement.

## Tolerancing motor hardware

| Feature | Control | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Case bore at a seal | AVG diameter; circularity restrained | Squeeze depends on the bore as the closure holds it |
| Case flange face | Flatness; perpendicularity to the case axis | Seal seating; closure squareness |
| Case flange threaded holes | Position at MMC, projected zone, to face, axis and clocking | Bolts must start and seat without bending |
| Closure seating face (datum A) | Flatness | Primary datum and face-seal land |
| Closure pilot (datum B) | Size; perpendicularity to A | Centres the closure and its seal groove |
| Closure bolt holes | Position at MMC to A, B at MMB, C | Assembly; gauged with pins |
| Seal groove root | Circular runout or position to B | Eccentricity eats squeeze |
| Groove walls | Parallelism or profile | Gland volume and ring stability |
| Domes, nozzle contour | Profile of a surface to the case datums | Shape, thickness and location at once |
| Ports and bosses | Position, and perpendicularity of their seal faces | Fitting alignment and sealing |

Anything not controlled on the drawing falls to the **general tolerances** in the title block (ISO 22081, or the older
ISO 2768-2). Those are coarse by design: class K of ISO 2768-2 allows 0.2 mm of runout on any round feature. Check every
feature the function depends on has its own control.

## ASME and ISO

| Topic | ASME Y14.5-2018 | ISO GPS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size and form | Rule #1: envelope by default | Independency by default (ISO 8015); Ⓔ for envelope |
| Concentricity, symmetry | Removed in 2018 | Still used |
| Exact dimensions | Basic dimensions | Theoretically exact dimensions |
| No modifier means | Regardless of feature size | The same |
| Datum modifiers | MMB, LMB on datum references | Ⓜ︎, Ⓛ on datum references (ISO 2692) |
| Non-rigid parts | Free state, restrained condition, AVG | ISO 10579 |

## Worked example — the aft closure, its bolts and the nozzle seal groove

The calculator's starting values in each tab are this example's.

1. **Datums for the closure.** The 30-bolt aft closure ([Bolted joints](../../joints/bolted-joints/#worked-example--the-aft-closure))
   seats on the case flange and is centred by a pilot in the case bore. So: **A**, the seating face, with its own
   flatness; **B**, the pilot diameter, perpendicular to A; **C**, a dowel hole for clocking.
2. **The bolt holes.** M10 bolts in ⌀11.0–11.2 holes on a basic 285 mm bolt circle: position ⌀0.5 at MMC to A, B at
   MMB, C. A hole made at ⌀11.15 earns a 0.15 bonus — ⌀0.65 allowed. Its axis measures 0.18 and 0.21 mm off true
   position: ⌀0.553. It conforms, **only because of the bonus**; the same error in a ⌀11.0 hole would be rejected,
   correctly, since a bolt might not pass. The functional gauge is 30 pins at the virtual condition, ⌀10.5.
3. **The threaded holes.** The bolts screw into the case flange through the 24 mm closure: fixed fasteners. The
   clearance is $11.0 - 10.0 = 1.0$, so ⌀0.5 for the closure holes leaves ⌀0.5 for the threaded holes — **with a
   projected tolerance zone 24 mm high**. Without it, over 15 mm of thread engagement, the threaded holes could have
   only ⌀0.12, or the closure holes would have to open to ⌀12.6, which the bolt heads and the seal land cannot spare.
4. **The nozzle seal groove.** The [O-ring page's](../../seals/o-ring-design/#worked-example--a-nozzle-to-case-radial-seal)
   radial seal: 150 mm bore, pilot ⌀149.860–149.900, groove root ⌀141.45–141.55. That page assumed the groove
   coaxial with its pilot, and found 10.8% minimum squeeze at end of life — just above its 10% floor. The drawing has
   to make that true. With **circular runout 0.05 of the groove root to B**, the gland grows from 4.408 to 4.433 mm at
   worst, and the same analysis gives **10.4%**. With no runout specified, the general tolerance allows 0.2: gland
   4.508 mm and **9.2%** — below the floor, on a part that passes every dimension on the drawing.
5. **The case.** A 1.377 mm wall on a 300 mm bore is not round until the pilot goes in. Specify the bore at the seal as
   an average diameter with circularity in the restrained condition — restrained by a plug gauge standing in for the
   closure pilot — and the flange face perpendicular to the case axis, which carries the closure's datum B.

## On the drawing

- **The governing standard** — ASME Y14.5-2018 or ISO GPS — named in the title block.
- **Datum features** chosen from assembly, each with its own form control.
- **Basic dimensions** for every true position and profile.
- **A geometric control on every feature the function depends on** — none left to the general tolerances by accident.
- **Projected zones** on threaded holes and press-fit pins.
- **Restraint notes** for non-rigid features, saying how the part is held for inspection.
