## Why this matters

Every pilot, seal bore, bearing seat, dowel and bushing on a motor is a fit. Too loose and a closure sits off-centre,
a seal extrudes or a pin does not locate; too tight and a part will not assemble, or a bearing runs hot, or a hub
cracks. And a fit chosen at 20 °C can do all of these at −40 or +60 °C when the two parts are made of different
materials.

ISO 286 turns the choice into a short code — *⌀150 H8/f7* — that every machinist, inspector and supplier reads the same
way, and that fixes both limits of both parts.

<Analogy title="A door in its frame">
  A door needs clearance to swing, but not so much that it rattles or lets the draught through. A good joiner hangs it
  for winter as well as summer, because wood swells in the damp. Choosing a fit is the same judgement: the gap the
  function needs, between limits the workshop can hold, in every season the part will see.
</Analogy>

## The system

A size is written as a **nominal size**, a **letter** and a **grade**:

- **The grade (IT1–IT18)** is the width of the tolerance zone. It grows with size and steps by about 1.6 times per
  grade — ten times every five grades. IT6–IT7 is precision turning and grinding, IT8–IT9 good turning, IT11 and coarser
  general machining.
- **The letter** is the **fundamental deviation**: where the zone sits relative to the nominal size. Upper case for holes,
  lower case for shafts. **H** holes and **h** shafts start exactly at the nominal size; a–g shafts sit below it
  (clearance), k–z above it (interference); js and JS straddle it.
- **Hole basis** — all holes H, the shafts varied — is the usual choice: reamers and plug gauges come in fixed sizes,
  shafts are easy to turn to any size. Shaft basis (h) suits a bar stock or a pin used through several holes.

Standard tolerances, µm, for a few sizes and grades:

| Size (mm) | IT6 | IT7 | IT8 | IT9 | IT11 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| over 18 to 30 | 13 | 21 | 33 | 52 | 130 |
| over 80 to 120 | 22 | 35 | 54 | 87 | 220 |
| over 120 to 180 | 25 | 40 | 63 | 100 | 250 |
| over 250 to 315 | 32 | 52 | 81 | 130 | 320 |

## Clearance, transition and interference

<FitZones />

With the hole's limits $EI$, $ES$ and the shaft's $ei$, $es$:

$$
c_{max} = ES - ei
$$

$$
c_{min} = EI - es
$$

Both positive is a **clearance** fit; both negative an **interference** fit; one of each a **transition** fit, which
may assemble either way. A common set, all on an H hole:

| Fit | Kind | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H11/c11, H9/d9 | Loose and free running | Coarse clearance, hot or dirty assemblies |
| H8/f7 | Close running | Running shafts, piston-type seal housings (ISO 3601) |
| H7/g6 | Sliding | Accurate location that still assembles by hand |
| H7/h6 | Locational clearance | Snug location of stationary parts |
| H7/k6 | Locational transition | Accurate location, light tapping to assemble |
| H7/n6 | Locational transition, tighter | Location that should not move |
| H7/p6 | Locational interference | Rigid location, removable with a press |
| H7/s6 | Medium drive | Bushings and sleeves that carry load by grip |

## Choosing fits for motor hardware

| Feature | Typical fit | What decides it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closure pilot in the case bore | H8/f7 to H7/g6 | Location and datum shift ([Datums & position](../datums-and-position/)); assembly over seals |
| Radial O-ring seal bore and plug | H8/f7 (ISO 3601-2) or closer | Extrusion gap is the largest clearance; eccentricity eats squeeze |
| Rolling-bearing seats | Shaft k5–m6, housing H7–J7, from the bearing maker | Which ring rotates under the load; bearing rings have their own tolerances (ISO 492) |
| Dowel pins | m6 pin in H7 on the fixed side; a looser hole on the removable side | Location without the pin carrying assembly load |
| Bushings in lugs and clevises | H7/p6 to H7/s6 | Grip against rotation and push-out; hub stress |
| Throat inserts and liners | Designed gaps, not a standard fit | Growth during firing — see [Transient thermal](../../motor/transient-thermal/) |

## Temperature changes the fit

ISO 286 limits apply at **20 °C** (ISO 1). Two parts of different materials change their fit with temperature:

$$
\Delta c = -\,d\,(\alpha_{shaft} - \alpha_{hole})\,(T - 20)
$$

- **An aluminium part in steel** tightens when hot and loosens when cold — a sliding fit can seize hot, an interference
  fit can go loose cold if the aluminium is inside, or hot if it is outside.
- **Same material** leaves the fit alone at uniform temperature; only transients change it.
- **Interference fits** give a contact pressure, hub stress and holding force from Lamé's thick-cylinder solution —
  see [Differential expansion](../../joints/differential-expansion/#fits-that-change-with-temperature). Check the
  tightest corner for stress and the loosest for grip.
- **Shrink fitting** heats the outer part (or cools the inner) by
  $\Delta T = (\delta_{max} + c_{assembly})/(\alpha\,d)$ so it slides on, then grips as it equalises.

## Worked example — four fits on the motor

The calculator's starting values are the first and last of these.

1. **The nozzle seal bore.** The [O-ring page's](../../seals/o-ring-design/#worked-example--a-nozzle-to-case-radial-seal)
   bore, ⌀150.000–150.063, is a ⌀150 **H8**. Its plug, ⌀149.860–149.900, gives up to 0.203 mm of clearance — which is
   why that page needed a back-up ring. The ISO 3601 housing fit, **H8/f7**, gives 0.043–0.146 mm: inside the
   roughly 0.15 mm a 90 Shore ring tolerates at 10 MPa, but not the 0.08 mm of a 70 Shore one. **H7/g6** gives
   0.014–0.079 mm, within both, and halves the eccentricity that eats squeeze
   ([GD&T](../gdt/#worked-example--the-aft-closure-its-bolts-and-the-nozzle-seal-groove)) — at the cost of IT6 and IT7
   machining and a careful lead-in chamfer for assembly over the ring.
2. **If the plug were aluminium.** In the steel case bore, H7/g6 at +60 °C becomes **0.054 mm of interference** — the
   nozzle seizes — and at −40 °C opens to **0.182 mm** of clearance, more than double the extrusion gap. Titanium would
   reverse the trend and still go slightly tight when cold. A close sliding fit wants matched materials.
3. **A dowel pin.** An ⌀8 m6 pin in an H7 hole: −0.015 to +0.009 mm, a transition fit that pushes or lightly presses in
   on the fixed side. On the removable side, F7 gives −0.002 to +0.022 mm — essentially a slip fit.
4. **The aluminium ring on the steel attach ring.** The ⌀310 joint from
   [Differential expansion](../../joints/differential-expansion/#worked-example--the-300-mm-motor-from-40-to-60-c),
   now as **H7/s6**: 0.118–0.202 mm of interference at 20 °C. At −40 °C, 0.330–0.414 mm — 4.5 MPa of contact pressure
   and 74 MPa in the aluminium, 15% of its yield. At +60 °C, −0.023 to +0.061 mm: **it can go loose**. As that page
   concluded, the fit only centres the ring; pins or bolts must carry the load. To assemble it, warm the ring about
   35 K above the steel.

## Where this stops

- **ISO 286 tables to 500 mm** for the common letters (d to s, D to S) and grades IT4–IT12. Larger sizes, other letters
  and J holes are in the standard.
- **Uniform temperature.** Firing transients heat parts unevenly — use [Transient thermal](../../motor/transient-thermal/)
  for the temperatures.
- **Elastic interference fits,** with no surface smoothing, no centrifugal loosening and no creep.
- **Form is separate.** Under ISO 8015 a size tolerance does not control form; apply the envelope requirement Ⓔ to
  fitting features, or rely on ASME's Rule #1 ([GD&T](../gdt/#size-and-form)).

## On the drawing

- **The fit as limits or as a code** — ⌀150 H8 and ⌀150 f7, or the limits themselves — on each part's drawing.
- **The envelope requirement** on fitting features under ISO GPS (⌀150 H8 Ⓔ).
- **Surface finish** on press and shrink fits, and lead-in chamfers wherever a part or a seal slides on.
- **The assembly method** for interference fits — press, heat or cool, and the temperatures.
- **Matched materials,** or the temperature range the fit was checked over.
