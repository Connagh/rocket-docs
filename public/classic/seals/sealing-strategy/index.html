Sealing strategy

In one lineDesign the joint so that the seal never has to follow a moving gap. Keep hot gas away from the seal. Give each primary seal an independent back-up, and a method to prove that the two seals work.
Before thisO-ring design · Bolted joints

Why this matters

Section titled “Why this matters”

Each joint in a motor is a possible leak path for gas at MEOP and at approximately 3000 K. The joints are case to closure, segment joints, nozzle to case, the igniter boss, and the pressure-transducer and test ports. A leak is seldom a slow loss of pressure. Hot gas cuts a channel through metal very quickly. The Challenger accident started at a booster field joint, where the O-rings could not follow the joint as it opened on a cold morning.

The O-ring page sizes one seal. This page is about the strategy: which seal, where, how many, how to check them, and how the joint keeps them working.

Bore or face? A bore seal sits on a spigot, so a flange lift has no effect on it. But all the radial growth of the bore has an effect on it. The bolts clamp a face seal, so radial growth has no effect on it. But it opens when the flange lifts or turns. Each type is the better choice where the other type is weak.

Think of it like…

A doorstop under a door that swells

A rubber wedge holds a door shut only while the gap stays at the size that it was fitted for. If the frame bends open, the wedge must expand to fill the gap, and a cold rubber wedge does not expand. If you make the frame stiff, the wedge never has to move.

Redundancy that is real

Section titled “Redundancy that is real”

Critical motor joints have two seals in series, a primary and a secondary, so that one can fail without a leak. This works only if the two do not fail together:

  • Prevent common causes. Two identical O-rings in the same gland region have the same cold, the same gap opening and the same contamination. They are two copies of one seal.
  • Add diversity where possible, for example a bore primary with a face secondary, or seals at different stiffness and temperature.
  • Verify the two seals. A secondary seal that was never tested is not verified. Do a leak check on each seal, or on the space between them, after assembly.
  • Count all the items in the path. Port plugs, transducer bosses and the threads of the igniter all need their own seal and back-up.

After Challenger, the redesigned field joint of the Shuttle booster added:

  • a capture feature that limits joint rotation;
  • a third O-ring;
  • an insulation flap that the pressure closes against the joint;
  • heaters to keep the seals warm.

Later, the nozzle joints also got a carbon-fibre rope thermal barrier in front of the O-rings.

Keeping the gland closed

Section titled “Keeping the gland closed”

The best seal is a seal whose gap never moves. Under pressure:

  • The bore grows. A thin-walled case grows radially by:
Δr=p r2E t(1−ν2)\Delta r = \frac{p\,r^2}{E\,t}\left(1 - \frac{\nu}{2}\right)Δr=Etpr2​(1−2ν​)

The growth is inversely proportional to the wall thickness at the seal seat. Put bore seals in a thick, stiff joint ring, not in the membrane.

  • The flange lifts or turns. Keep the bolted joint well below separation (Bolted joints), make the flanges stiff, and put face seals inboard of the bolts.
  • The spigot can help. If the mating part grows with the case, the gap stays closed.
  • Temperature has a large effect. A cold elastomer recovers slowly and shrinks. Keep the seals in their qualified range, with heaters or insulation if necessary (Transient thermal).
  • Keep hot gas away from the seal. Insulation, joint fillers and thermal barriers stop gas and particles before they get to the O-ring. The O-ring then only has to hold pressure.

Leak-check ports

Section titled “Leak-check ports”

A port drilled into the space between two seals lets you apply pressure and monitor the pressure decrease:

Q=V Δpt(in standard cm3/s with Δp in atmospheres)Q = \frac{V\,\Delta p}{t} \quad\text{(in standard cm}^3\text{/s with } \Delta p \text{ in atmospheres)}Q=tVΔp​(in standard cm3/s with Δp in atmospheres)
  • Temperature changes look like leaks. The pressure of the trapped gas changes by pabs ΔT/Tp_{abs}\,\Delta T/Tpabs​ΔT/T. A change of one tenth of a degree can be larger than the resolution of the gauge. Make the temperature stable and record it, or use a helium mass-spectrometer test for smaller limits.
  • The direction is important. Pressure between two seals pushes the primary seal from its back, onto the incorrect groove wall. At ignition, it must move across before it seals. Where possible, test in the operating direction. The Rogers Commission also found that a higher leak-check pressure on the Shuttle field joint could blow holes in its insulating putty.
  • Plug the port and verify the plug. After the test, the port is part of the pressure boundary. It needs a sealed plug, its own torque and its own check.

    Worked example: the aft closure seal

    Section titled “Worked example: the aft closure seal”

    The aft closure of the 300 mm case seals on a radial O-ring with a 5.33 mm section. It has a minimum squeeze of 10.8% at the cold condition after compression set (O-ring design). MEOP is 10 MPa, and the closure spigot grows by approximately 0.02 mm.

    1. Seat in the plain cylinder (1.37 mm wall, from Case sizing). The radius of the case grows by 0.68 mm, which is its full hoop strain of 0.45%. The gland opens by 0.66 mm, but the ring is compressed by only 0.58 mm. Thus the seal unloads and leaks. Never put a bore seal in the membrane.
    2. Seat in a 10 mm joint ring. The growth is 0.094 mm and the gland opens by 0.074 mm. The squeeze is 9.4%, which is less than a 10% minimum.
    3. The seat wall for 10% is 15.0 mm. Thus the joint ring is a 15 mm forging. Some hundred grams make the seal work.
    4. Redundancy: two rings with a port between them. With 2 cm³ between the seals, 700 kPa, a 0.1 kPa gauge and 10 minutes, the smallest leak that you can see is 3.3×10−63.3\times10^{-6}3.3×10−6 std cm³/s. But a drift of 0.1 °C moves the pressure by 0.27 kPa, which is more than the gauge resolution. Thus temperature control decides the result. For a permitted leak of 1×10−51\times10^{-5}1×10−5 std cm³/s (an example), a 200 s hold is sufficient with a stable temperature.
    5. Diversity: the bolted closure flange from Bolted joints does not separate at MEOP (17.7 kN against 32.1 kN). Thus a face seal at that flange would stay closed. The same mechanism cannot open a bore primary and a face secondary.

    Where this stops

    Section titled “Where this stops”
    • Thin-wall growth only. At the joint ring, the junction with the dome or the next segment bends and turns. To get the real gap opening, you need a joint analysis or a finite-element model.
    • Static. At ignition, the gap opens in milliseconds. If a cold ring can follow is a viscoelastic question.
    • Simplified pressure-decay physics: ideal gas and constant volume. Helium permeation through elastomers sets a limit on very sensitive helium tests.
    • No model of erosion and hot-gas flow through a gap. Keep the gas away from the seals.

    On the drawing

    Section titled “On the drawing”
    • Seals and back-up rings by specification: size, compound, hardness and lot traceability.
    • Gland dimensions and surface finish, and the lubricant.
    • Seal seat wall and joint stiffness: the dimensions that the gap analysis used.
    • Leak-test procedure: pressure, medium, direction, hold time, temperature record and acceptance.
    • Port plugs: seal, torque and verification. Replace the seals each time that the joint is opened.
