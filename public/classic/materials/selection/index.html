Selecting materials

In one lineRank materials by the combination of properties that the function needs (a performance index), not by the largest number on a datasheet.
Leads toSpecifying materials · Case sizing

Why this matters

Section titled “Why this matters”

The material selection usually has the largest single effect on mass, cost and risk. It is made early, when a change costs little. It is also the most frequent source of design errors, for a simple reason: designs are sized with the incorrect number. The typical room-temperature yield on a datasheet is not the minimum strength at the service temperature, in the load direction, for the product form that you can buy. This page is about how to make the correct comparison first. The specification page is about how to fix the result on a drawing.

The idea: a performance index

Section titled “The idea: a performance index”

Think of it like…

Choosing a minibus by fuel used per passenger

If you must move eight people, the car with the best fuel consumption is not the correct selection. A two-seat sports car would win on that value. The important value is the fuel consumption for each seat: the combination of properties that the function needs. A performance index is the same idea for materials. For a light, strong pressure vessel, it is strength divided by density, not strength alone.

The Ashby method changes a design requirement into that index in four steps:

  1. Function: what does the part do? (Contain pressure, carry a bending load, conduct heat.)
  2. Objective: what do you minimise or maximise? (Usually mass, sometimes cost or deflection.)
  3. Constraints: what must the part always do? (Not yield at proof, not burst, survive 150 °C, be weldable.)
  4. Free variables: what can you change? (Wall thickness, section size, the material.)

Write the objective. Use the constraint to remove the free variable. The remaining term is a group of material properties. Maximise that group.

This is a line with a slope of 2. All the materials on that line have the same performance for the function. All the materials above it are better. This is the dashed guideline on the chart below.

Think of it like…

Contour lines on a map

A property chart is a map of all materials. The families are countries: metals in one area, polymers lower down, and composites in a long thin island. A performance index is a set of contour lines across the full map. If you move along one line, the material changes but the performance does not. If you move uphill, perpendicular to the lines, the part becomes better.

Fracture needs its own index

Section titled “Fracture needs its own index”

The strength-for-weight index gives the best score to the strongest alloys. But toughness usually decreases as strength increases. Two fracture indices show the other side:

  • Yield before break, KIC/σyK_{IC}/\sigma_yKIC​/σy​. The critical crack size at yield changes with its square: ac=1π(KICYσy)2a_c = \frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{K_{IC}}{Y\sigma_y}\right)^2ac​=π1​(Yσy​KIC​​)2. A large value is more tolerant.
  • Leak before break, KIC2/σyK_{IC}^2/\sigma_yKIC2​/σy​. This gives the largest wall that a pressure vessel can have, and still leak before it breaks (see the leak-before-break screen).

Alone, these indices select soft, tough metals that are not suitable for a light case. When you use them together with the mass index, they show the real trade: how much toughness you lose for each kilogram that you save.

  2. Check fracture. The lightest selection, maraging 300, tolerates a crack of only approximately half a millimetre at yield. This is near the limit of reliable inspection. Maraging 250 loses 10% of the mass index, but it tolerates a crack almost two times larger. Ti-6Al-4V ELI loses 20% of the mass index against maraging 300, but it tolerates a crack almost eight times deeper. These numbers show the yield-before-break trade.

  3. Check what the chart cannot show. D6AC is eighth on mass and low on toughness, but it was used on many large motors. The reasons are not on the chart. Large forgings and plate are available, the heat treatment and welding are well known, the indicative price in the database is approximately a quarter to a third of that of maraging steel, and many years of test data support it. Titanium has cost and availability problems in large sections. Maraging steels have price and supply risk from cobalt.

  4. Composites. Select the composites family. Filament-wound carbon/epoxy is far above all the metals, but this is the strength in the fibre direction. A real filament-wound case is a laminate, and the strength of its wall is a fraction of that number. It also has its own failure modes (matrix cracking, impact damage, stress rupture). But it usually gives the lowest mass. This is why many modern cases are composite.

What the example shows

An index ranks. It does not decide. Use the mass index to find the candidates, and a second index to show their cost in other properties. Use engineering judgement for all the items that the chart cannot plot: supply, process, cost, compatibility and previous use.

Where this stops

Section titled “Where this stops”

Screening data, not allowables

The chart uses typical room-temperature values from REF·DB v1. Designs are sized with statistically based allowables (for example MMPDS A-basis or B-basis) for the specific product form, thickness, grain direction and temperature, from the approved data of your organisation.

  • Temperature. The strength and toughness at the service temperature, also after any thermal exposure, decide the selection. The maximum-temperature filter is only a screen.
  • Product form and direction. Plate, forging and extrusion are different. The short-transverse direction of thick plate can be much weaker and less tough than the rolling direction. See specifying materials.
  • Environment. Stress-corrosion cracking, hydrogen embrittlement, and compatibility with propellant, fluids and coatings remove candidates that the chart ranks high.
  • Cost and availability are real constraints that you must consider from the start. They also change.
