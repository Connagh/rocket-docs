GD&T

In one lineGD&T tells what each feature must do relative to the features that it mates with. Where the function permits, it gives more tolerance.
Before thisBolted joints · O-ring design
Used byDatums & position · Tolerance stack

Why this matters

Section titled “Why this matters”

A dimension with a ± tolerance tells the size of a feature and its approximate position. It does not tell how flat, round, square or coaxial the feature must be, or where to measure it from. Each inspector, machinist and supplier then makes a different assumption. Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T) removes this problem. It controls each feature relative to datums, which you select from how the part fits and operates. The tolerance zones have the shape that the function needs.

On a motor, GD&T can decide if a seal keeps its squeeze, if all 30 bolts of a closure start, and if a nozzle is on the axis. It also decreases cost. It gives back the tolerance that the function does not need, and it often does this automatically as features move away from their tightest size.

Think of it like…

Darts

The score of a dart depends on its distance from the bull, not on separate distances to the left and up. A ± tolerance on x and y is a square box. It rejects a dart in the corner of the circle that would have scored, and it accepts no dart that it must reject. A position tolerance is the circle. For the same worst case, it gives 57% more area.

The geometric characteristics

Section titled “The geometric characteristics”

Two rules help you select between them. First, use the smallest control that makes sure of the function: flatness where a face only seals, perpendicularity where it must also be square to an axis, and profile where it must also be in the correct position. Second, use controls that a gauge or a measuring machine can check against the same datums that the assembly uses.

The feature control frame

Section titled “The feature control frame”

You read the frame as a sentence: the position of these holes must be in a ⌀0.5 cylinder at MMC, relative to datums A, B at MMB, and C. These are the modifiers that are most frequently important on motor hardware:

  • Ⓜ︎ Maximum material condition. The tolerance increases as the feature moves away from its tightest size. Use it for clearance: bolt holes, and pilots that only have to assemble.
  • Ⓛ Least material condition. The opposite. Use it to protect a minimum wall or edge distance.
  • No modifier: regardless of feature size. This is the default. Use it for features that locate accurately.
  • Ⓟ Projected tolerance zone. The zone extends above a threaded hole, where the fastener is.
  • Ⓕ Free state. Use it for non-rigid parts that you measure without restraint.

Datums

Section titled “Datums”

A datum feature is a real surface on the part. The datum is the perfect plane, axis or point that it gives in the gauge or fixture that touches it (the datum feature simulator). Three datums in order of precedence make a datum reference frame:

  • Primary. The part sits on it at three points, which fixes three degrees of freedom. It is usually the largest mating face.
  • Secondary. Two points, or the axis of a pilot, which fix two more degrees of freedom.
  • Tertiary. One point, or a clocking hole, which fixes the last degree of freedom.

Select datums from assembly and function, not from what is easy in the machine shop. Examples are the face that a closure sits on, the pilot that centres it, and the dowel that clocks it. If a datum feature has a loose control, all the features that refer to it are also loose. Thus the datum features get their own form controls (flatness of A, and perpendicularity of B to A). If a secondary datum such as a pilot is at maximum material boundary (MMB), the pattern can also shift by the clearance of the pilot. This tolerance is only applicable if the mating part really has that clearance.

Size and form

Section titled “Size and form”
  • ASME: Rule #1. If the drawing does not say differently, a feature of size must have perfect form at MMC. The size limits also limit the form (the envelope principle).
  • ISO: independency. Under ISO 8015, size and form are independent unless the envelope requirement Ⓔ is applied. The same drawing can have different meanings in the two systems. Tell which system applies.
  • Non-rigid parts. A thin case wall is not round in the free state. It becomes round when the closure pilot goes in. Control it in the condition where it operates: an average diameter (AVG) and circularity in the restrained condition, with free-state limits marked Ⓕ, and a note that tells how to restrain it for inspection (ASME Y14.5; ISO 10579).

MMC, bonus tolerance and virtual condition

Section titled “MMC, bonus tolerance and virtual condition”

    Worked example: the aft closure, its bolts and the nozzle seal groove

    Section titled “Worked example: the aft closure, its bolts and the nozzle seal groove”

    The starting values in each tab of the calculator are the values of this example.

    1. Datums for the closure. The 30-bolt aft closure (Bolted joints) sits on the case flange, and a pilot in the case bore centres it. Thus: A is the seating face, with its own flatness. B is the pilot diameter, perpendicular to A. C is a dowel hole for clocking.
    2. The bolt holes. M10 bolts go in ⌀11.0–11.2 holes on a basic 285 mm bolt circle, with position ⌀0.5 at MMC to A, B at MMB, C. A hole made at ⌀11.15 gets a 0.15 bonus, so ⌀0.65 is permitted. Its axis is 0.18 mm and 0.21 mm from true position, which is ⌀0.553. The hole conforms only because of the bonus. The same error in a ⌀11.0 hole would be rejected, and this is correct, because a bolt possibly could not go through. The functional gauge has 30 pins at the virtual condition, ⌀10.5.
    3. The threaded holes. The bolts go through the 24 mm closure into threads in the case flange, so they are fixed fasteners. The clearance is 11.0−10.0=1.011.0 - 10.0 = 1.011.0−10.0=1.0. If the closure holes get ⌀0.5, the threaded holes also get ⌀0.5, with a projected tolerance zone 24 mm high. Without this zone and with 15 mm of thread engagement, the threaded holes can have only ⌀0.12. Alternatively, the closure holes must increase to ⌀12.6, and the bolt heads and the seal land do not have space for this.
    4. The nozzle seal groove. This is the radial seal of the O-ring page: a 150 mm bore, a pilot of ⌀149.860–149.900 and a groove root of ⌀141.45–141.55. That page assumed that the groove is coaxial with its pilot. It found a minimum squeeze of 10.8% at the end of life, which is just above its 10% minimum. The drawing must make this assumption true. With a circular runout of 0.05 for the groove root to B, the worst gland increases from 4.408 to 4.433 mm, and the same analysis gives 10.4%. If the drawing gives no runout, the general tolerance permits 0.2. The gland is then 4.508 mm and the squeeze is 9.2%. This is below the minimum, on a part that passes all the dimensions on the drawing.
    5. The case. A 1.377 mm wall on a 300 mm bore is not round until the pilot goes in. Specify the bore at the seal as an average diameter, with circularity in the restrained condition. A plug gauge restrains the bore in place of the closure pilot. Also specify the flange face as perpendicular to the case axis, because it carries datum B of the closure.

    Where this stops

    Section titled “Where this stops”
    • An introduction to the language. Datum schemes, composite and pattern position, profile with datum shift, and inspection planning are on Datums & position.
    • One feature at a time. How tolerances add across an assembly is a tolerance stack.
    • ASME Y14.5 conventions in the calculator. ISO is different in some places. Check which standard your drawing uses.
    • Squeeze from geometry only in the gland tab. The full seal check is on O-ring design.

    On the drawing

    Section titled “On the drawing”
    • The applicable standard (ASME Y14.5-2018 or ISO GPS) in the title block.
    • Datum features selected from the assembly, each with its own form control.
    • Basic dimensions for each true position and profile.
    • A geometric control on each feature that the function needs. Do not leave one to the general tolerances by accident.
    • Projected zones on threaded holes and press-fit pins.
    • Restraint notes for non-rigid features, which tell how to hold the part for inspection.
