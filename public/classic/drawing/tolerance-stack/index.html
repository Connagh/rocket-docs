Tolerance stack

In one lineA stack follows one gap through all the parts that set it. The worst case adds all the tolerances. A statistical stack adds them as real parts vary. Which method you can use is a decision about risk.
Before thisFits & limits · Datums & position
Used byO-ring design · Gauging

Why this matters

Section titled “Why this matters”

All the drawings of a motor can be correct and the assembly can still be incorrect. An O-ring groove, a bore and a pilot can each be in tolerance, and the ring can still have too little squeeze. A case, a closure and a nozzle can each pass inspection, and the thrust can still point away from the centre of gravity. The individual drawings do not show this. The stack shows it. A stack is the chain of dimensions that sets one gap.

A stack answers three questions:

  • What values can the gap have?
  • Which tolerances have the largest effect on it?
  • Must the result be correct for all assemblies, or only for almost all of them?

Think of it like…

Books on a shelf

Each book on a shelf is a little thicker or thinner than the catalogue says. If you take books at random, most of the errors cancel and the row ends near where you expect. The row is too long only if all the books are thick ones. This is possible, but rare. If someone sorted the books, or if all of them came from one print run, the errors add and the rare result occurs each time. A tolerance stack does the same three things: it adds the worst case, it adds the usual case, and it tells you which one your parts permit.

The loop diagram

Section titled “The loop diagram”
  1. Name the result and its requirement. The result is a gap, a squeeze or an offset. The requirement is the limit that it must meet.
  2. Start at one side of the gap. Go to the other side through the parts that touch, with one arrow for each dimension. Select a positive direction. Arrows in the opposite direction are negative.
  3. Go through datums and contact points. The dimensions of each part start at its datums, and parts meet where they touch. Thus the loop goes from datum to datum. If the drawing gives a dimension from a different feature, the loop must go a longer way and it collects more tolerance. This usually shows that the datums are not correct (Datums & position).
  4. Decide the position of each clearance. If a fit lets a part move, push the part in the direction that is worst for this result. The loop then goes through the contact point.
  5. Change each dimension to a mean ± an equal half-tolerance. Give it a sensitivity: ±1 for a length in the loop, ±½ for a diameter whose radius is in the loop, and cos⁡θ\cos\thetacosθ for a dimension at an angle.

The O-ring page has a nozzle seal. Push the plug onto the far wall of the bore, and let the groove move by its runout eee. The deepest gland is then:

g=Dbore−12Dpilot−12Droot+eg = D_{bore} - \tfrac{1}{2}D_{pilot} - \tfrac{1}{2}D_{root} + eg=Dbore​−21​Dpilot​−21​Droot​+e

The squeeze is the ring section minus ggg. On the tight side, the bore diameter is not in the stack, because the plug sits on the bore.

Worst case and RSS

Section titled “Worst case and RSS”

The result is y=∑sixiy = \sum s_i x_iy=∑si​xi​. Each contributor has a half-tolerance tit_iti​ and a sensitivity sis_isi​:

tWC=∑∣si∣ tit_{WC} = \sum |s_i|\,t_itWC​=∑∣si​∣ti​ tRSS=∑(siti)2t_{RSS} = \sqrt{\textstyle\sum (s_i t_i)^2}tRSS​=∑(si​ti​)2​
  • Worst case. The result is correct for all assemblies of in-tolerance parts, for all distributions. It increases in proportion to the number of contributors.
  • RSS. This method assumes that each part is independent, normal and centred, and that its tolerance is ±3σ. It increases only as the square root of the number of contributors. Thus it is much smaller on long stacks. Even when all the assumptions are correct, approximately 0.27% of assemblies are outside the RSS band.
  • Which method to use. This is a decision. Use the worst case when one failure is not acceptable and the assembly check will not find it, when the quantity is small, or when you do not have process data for the statistics. A statistical stack is applicable to many contributors, to large quantities, and to failures that an inspection finds or that are acceptable at the predicted rate.

Statistical stacks: Cpk and Monte Carlo

Section titled “Statistical stacks: Cpk and Monte Carlo”

Cpk-based. Real processes do not all have a Cpk of 1. For a part made at a capability CpkC_{pk}Cpk​, a conservative value is σi=ti/(3 Cpk,i)\sigma_i = t_i / (3\,C_{pk,i})σi​=ti​/(3Cpk,i​). The σ and the capability of the assembly follow, where μ is its mean:

σy=∑(siσi)2\sigma_y = \sqrt{\textstyle\sum (s_i \sigma_i)^2}σy​=∑(si​σi​)2​ Cpk,y=min⁡(USL−μ, μ−LSL)3 σyC_{pk,y} = \frac{\min(USL - \mu,\ \mu - LSL)}{3\,\sigma_y}Cpk,y​=3σy​min(USL−μ, μ−LSL)​

Plain RSS is the special case where each CpkC_{pk}Cpk​ is 1. If a process is off-centre (Cpk<CpC_{pk} < C_pCpk​<Cp​), this method treats the shift as random. If the shift is the same on all parts, add it as a worst case. Some parts are uniform across their tolerance, for example because of tool wear between resets, or because nobody measures the feature. For these parts, σ=t/3\sigma = t/\sqrt{3}σ=t/3​, which is 1.7 times the normal value. If you do not know the capability of the parts, a traditional allowance multiplies the RSS by 1.5 (Bender).

Monte Carlo. Take a sample of each part from its distribution. Calculate the result with the real function, also if it is nonlinear (for example a squeeze as a percentage of the sampled section, or a vector sum). Then count the failures. This method does not need a normal assumption, but it needs sufficient samples. To see a failure rate of 1 in 10,000, use some hundred thousand samples. The result is only as good as the distributions that you use.

Correlation. RSS and Monte Carlo assume that the parts are independent. Two features made in one setup, by one tool or from one batch, change together. Put them in a group and add them as a worst case.

Radial stacks

Section titled “Radial stacks”

Coaxiality, position and clearance shifts are offsets in a plane, in any direction. They add as vectors:

  • The worst case is ∑ri\sum r_i∑ri​, with all offsets in one line.
  • The RSS of the radii, ∑ri2\sqrt{\sum r_i^2}∑ri2​​, is not a limit. It treats each offset as a ±3σ dimension. A clearance that is fully taken up in a random direction is very different. With two equal clearances, half of all assemblies are more than this value.
  • Many random offsets approach a Rayleigh distribution, with σ2=∑σi2\sigma^2 = \sum \sigma_i^2σ2=∑σi2​ in each direction. Use r2/4r^2/4r2/4 for an offset anywhere in a zone, and r2/2r^2/2r2/2 for a clearance that is fully taken up. The radius that contains a fraction ppp is then σ−2ln⁡(1−p)\sigma\sqrt{-2\ln(1-p)}σ−2ln(1−p)​. With few offsets, use Monte Carlo.
  • A common direction makes random offsets add arithmetically. On a horizontal assembly stand, gravity takes up each clearance in the downward direction. A side load during bolt tightening has the same effect.
  • Tilts are also offsets. A tolerance ttt over a length lll tilts an axis by t/lt/lt/l. At a lever arm LLL, this moves the axis by tL/ltL/ltL/l.

For a nozzle, the important result is the offset ddd of the thrust line from the centre of gravity. This offset gives a disturbing moment M=FdM = F dM=Fd, which the vehicle control system must correct. It also combines the lateral offset and the tilt of the nozzle into one value.

Offsets: name, largest offset, kind

Write a tilt as “t/l”, a tolerance over the length it acts on; it is carried to the lever arm.

    Linear stacks: worst case by adding half tolerances; RSS with each normal part centred and its tolerance at ±3·Cpk σ, uniform parts at the half tolerance over √3; Monte Carlo of 100,000 assemblies with a fixed seed. Radial stacks: vector sums, Monte Carlo of 100,000. Independent contributors throughout.

    Worked example: seal squeeze and nozzle alignment

    Section titled “Worked example: seal squeeze and nozzle alignment”

    The starting values of the calculator are the values of this example. The part Cpk of 1.33 and the two requirements are for this example only. Push Load worked example to apply them.

    The nozzle seal

    Section titled “The nozzle seal”

    This is the radial seal of the O-ring page, with the groove runout that the GD&T page added to the drawing:

    ArrowLimits (mm)SensitivityMean ± half
    O-ring section5.20–5.46+15.330 ± 0.130
    Case bore ⌀ (H8)150.000–150.063−1150.0315 ± 0.0315
    Plug pilot ⌀149.860–149.900+½149.880 ± 0.020
    Groove root ⌀141.45–141.55+½141.500 ± 0.050
    Root runout 0.05 to the pilot(none)−10 ± 0.025
    1. Nominal and worst case. The nominal squeeze is 0.989 mm. The worst case is 0.767 mm, which is 14.75% of the smallest ring and agrees with the GD&T page. The requirement in this example is 0.74 mm. At this value, the stretch, the −40 °C temperature and the 20% compression set of the O-ring page leave its 10% minimum. The worst case passes, by 0.027 mm.
    2. RSS. The band is ±0.139 mm, so the low end is 0.850 mm. The section of the O-ring itself is 88% of the variance. If you halve all four machined tolerances, the result changes by less than 0.01 mm. A ring with a section tolerance of ±0.08 changes it by approximately 0.05 mm. The machining does not control this stack.
    3. Cpk. With all parts at Cpk 1.33, σ is 0.035 mm. The Cpk of the assembly against the 0.74 mm minimum is 2.38. None of the 100,000 Monte Carlo assemblies is below the minimum.
    4. A possible cost saving. Change the runout to the general tolerance of 0.2. The worst case is then 0.692 mm. This is 9.2% at the end of life, which is below the minimum. RSS gives 0.819 mm, which passes. Nobody measures a general tolerance, thus use a uniform distribution for the runout (“-0.1, 0.1, u”). The runout is then 74% of the variance and the Cpk of the assembly decreases to 1.23. The Monte Carlo finds 1 assembly in 100,000 below the minimum. The normal estimate gives 1 in 10,000. A uniform part has no tails, and the normal approximation does not show this.
    5. Decision: use the worst case and keep the 0.05. At assembly, you cannot see a seal that has too little squeeze. Its failure causes a burn-through, and the quantity made is small. The statistics would accept a risk that the programme must not accept. The runout has no cost if the groove and the pilot are machined in one setup.

    The nozzle alignment

    Section titled “The nozzle alignment”

    The result is the offset of the thrust line at the centre of gravity of the vehicle. At ignition, this point is 700 mm forward of the aft joint (illustrative value). The thrust is 29.5 kN, from the load-path page. The closure pilot fit is from the datums page. The other tolerances are illustrative. The requirement is that 99.73% of motors are within 0.45 mm.

    OffsetToleranceKindAt the CG (mm)
    Case flange bore to the case axiscoaxiality ⌀0.10zone0.050
    Case flange faceperpendicularity 0.05 over ⌀300zone, tilt0.117

    1. Worst case: 0.610 mm. This gives 18.0 N·m, which is the same as a thrust tilt of 0.050°. It does not meet 0.45 mm. The two squareness tilts and the closure clearance are 58% of the total.
    2. RSS of the radii: 0.246 mm. This seems to pass easily, but 26% of motors are more than this value. Do not use it.
    3. Monte Carlo, vertical assembly. The two clearance shifts are in random directions. 99.73% of motors are within 0.442 mm (13.1 N·m). This passes by a small margin: 0.20% are more than 0.45 mm.
    4. Horizontal assembly. The nozzle and the closure move to the bottom of their clearances before the bolts are torqued. Change the two shifts to “fixed”. The 99.73% value increases to 0.474 mm, and 0.81% of motors are more than the limit. The stack fails, but no part has changed.
    5. The solution. Change the nozzle pilot to H7/g6 (Fits & limits), which gives a 0.0395 mm shift. Then 99.73% of motors are within 0.415 mm, also with horizontal assembly. Another solution is to centre the nozzle in a fixture during torquing. After the change, do the seal stack again, because the plug pilot is one of its arrows. Also measure the nozzle alignment of each motor. Inspection is the best way to show that a statistical requirement is met.

    Where this stops

    Section titled “Where this stops”
    • Independent contributors. You must put correlated features (one setup, one tool, one batch) in groups yourself.
    • Linear sensitivities in the linear tab. Nonlinear results need their own function. The Monte Carlo in the library permits this.
    • One lever arm for all tilts in the radial tab. Tilts about points far apart need their own lever arms. Also, the centre of gravity moves as the propellant burns.
    • Geometry only. Loads, pressure, deflection and temperature also change gaps. See the joint opening on the O-ring page, and Differential expansion for temperature. Thrust misalignment also comes from the flow and from nozzle erosion. Tolerances do not include these.
    • Normal and uniform parts only. Real distributions can be skewed, cut off by inspection, or have two peaks from two machines. Use a separate Monte Carlo with measured data for these.

    On the drawing

    Section titled “On the drawing”
    • Datums that make the loop short. Give each critical dimension from the datum that the stack uses.
    • All the tolerances that the stack uses. Do not leave one to the general tolerances by accident, as could occur with the groove runout.
    • Assembly instructions where the stack assumes a position: centring, orientation and the tightening sequence.
    • The stack in the design record: the loop, the method, the distributions that you assumed and the data for them.
    • Inspection of the assembled result where the requirement is statistical: measure or gauge the alignment, the gap or the squeeze.
