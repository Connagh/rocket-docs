How a solid rocket motor works

In one lineA solid motor is a pressure vessel full of fuel, with one carefully sized hole in it.
You’ll learnWhat each part does, why the chamber pressure controls itself, and why small changes become large changes.
Leads toCase & pressure vessel sizing

The whole idea in one picture

Section titled “The whole idea in one picture”

A solid rocket motor has no pumps, valves or moving parts. The fuel and the oxidiser are mixed into a solid, rubber-like grain, which is cast directly into the case. After ignition, the exposed surface burns and makes hot gas. The gas pressurises the case, and it can go out only through the nozzle. All the properties of the motor come from this arrangement.

Think of it like…

A pressure cooker that is also the stove

The case is a pressure cooker, the grain is the fuel that burns inside it, and the nozzle throat is the valve on the lid. The difference from a kitchen pressure cooker is that here the “stove” burns faster as the pressure increases. The result can be a steady hiss or an explosion. One number decides which, and the remainder of this page is about that number.

How the propellant burns

Section titled “How the propellant burns”

Composite propellant burns only at its exposed surface. That surface moves back perpendicular to itself at the burn rate rrr, which is usually 5–20 mm/s for tactical and booster propellants. The burn rate depends strongly on the chamber pressure. Over a useful range, it follows the law of Saint-Robert (Vieille):

r=a pc nr = a\,p_c^{\,n}r=apcn​
  • aaa is the burn-rate coefficient, set by the propellant formulation and its initial temperature.
  • nnn is the pressure exponent, usually approximately 0.3–0.5 for AP/HTPB propellants.

Think of it like…

Toast under a grill

Burning propellant is similar to bread under a grill. Only the surface that faces the heat changes, and it moves in layer by layer. If you make the grill hotter (increase the pressure), the bread becomes brown faster, but not proportionally faster. The exponent n<1n < 1n<1 gives this “not proportionally”.

The surface moves back everywhere at the same time. Thus the shape of the bore decides how the burning area AbA_bAb​ changes as the grain burns. A plain circular bore increases in area as it opens (progressive thrust). A star bore or a slotted bore can be shaped to keep the area almost constant (neutral thrust). Grain design sets the thrust–time curve of the motor.

Why chamber pressure settles at one value

Section titled “Why chamber pressure settles at one value”

The burning area and the burn rate set the rate at which gas is made. The throat area and the chamber pressure set the rate at which gas goes out through the choked throat. The pressure increases until the two rates are equal:

Here ρp\rho_pρp​ is the propellant density, AtA_tAt​ is the throat area, and c∗c^*c∗ is the characteristic velocity. c∗c^*c∗ measures how much hot gas the propellant makes for each unit of throat flow. The ratio Kn=Ab/AtK_n = A_b/A_tKn​=Ab​/At​ is the main design parameter.

Think of it like…

A bath with the tap on and the plug out

Water flows in from the tap and out through the plug hole. The level increases until the outflow, which increases with depth, is equal to the inflow. A solid motor does the same with pressure in place of water level. The difference is that in a motor, the tap opens more as the level increases (pnp^npn). While the plug hole “wins” (n<1n<1n<1), the level still becomes stable. If the tap won (n≥1n \geq 1n≥1), the bath would overflow, for any size of plug hole.

The consequence for hardware: small changes are amplified

Section titled “The consequence for hardware: small changes are amplified”

The balance shows how a fractional change in burning area, burn rate or throat area becomes a change in pressure:

Δpcpc≈11−n ΔKnKn\frac{\Delta p_c}{p_c} \approx \frac{1}{1-n}\,\frac{\Delta K_n}{K_n}pc​Δpc​​≈1−n1​Kn​ΔKn​​

With n=0.4n = 0.4n=0.4, a 1% change in burning area changes the pressure by approximately 1.7%. Because of this amplification, the maximum expected operating pressure (MEOP), which is the design pressure of the case, is well above the nominal pressure. It must cover all of these items together:

  • Hot propellant. The burn rate increases with the initial grain temperature. The pressure sensitivity πK=(∂ln⁡pc/∂T)Kn\pi_K = \left(\partial \ln p_c / \partial T\right)_{K_n}πK​=(∂lnpc​/∂T)Kn​​ is usually approximately 0.1–0.3 %/°C. Thus the difference between a cold-soaked motor and a hot-soaked motor can be tens of per cent.
  • Manufacturing variation in burn rate, grain geometry and throat diameter.
  • The ignition spike and any erosive burning early in the burn, when the gas speeds along the bore are highest.

Hardware designers get MEOP, they do not set it

MEOP is a ballistics output that already includes these effects. A frequent and expensive error is to size a case for the nominal pressure, and to find later that the hot-day value is 15–25% higher.

From pressure to thrust

Section titled “From pressure to thrust”

The thrust is the chamber pressure on the throat area, multiplied by the thrust coefficient CFC_FCF​ of the nozzle:

F=CF pc AtF = C_F\, p_c\, A_tF=CF​pc​At​

CFC_FCF​ is usually approximately 1.3–1.8. It depends on the expansion ratio of the nozzle and on the ambient pressure. It measures how well the nozzle changes pressure into exhaust momentum. Thus the thrust follows the chamber pressure trace, which follows the burning area, which follows the grain shape.

What to take away

Section titled “What to take away”
  1. The pressure controls itself only because n<1n < 1n<1. Propellants are selected for a low, stable nnn.
  2. KnK_nKn​ sets the pressure. The grain geometry sets AbA_bAb​ during the burn. Throat erosion slowly increases AtA_tAt​.
  3. Changes are amplified by 1/(1−n)1/(1-n)1/(1−n). Thus temperature, tolerances and transients must all be within MEOP.
  4. The case must withstand MEOP, proof and burst. The page case & pressure vessel sizing continues from here. For what each hardware part carries, how it fails and what its mass is, see rocket motor hardware.
